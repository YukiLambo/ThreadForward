Our mission

Fashion has
a future.
We're wearing it.

ThreadForward makes clothing reuse practical and engaging within HKBU and the local community — through a donation booth, hands-on workshops and a free campus rack.

Fast fashion is an industry model built on producing cheap, on-trend clothing quickly, encouraging people to buy items for brief wear and quick disposal (Bick et al., 2018; Niinimäki et al., 2020). By promoting frequent purchases and short use cycles, it has driven unnecessary textile waste.

Much of that waste is avoidable. Usable garments are thrown out over minor defects or a lack of repair know-how — and many of us don't fully see how everyday buying choices connect to wider environmental sustainability.

400tof textiles go into Hong Kong's landfills every day.

According to the Environmental Protection Department (2025), Hong Kong disposes of an average of 400 tonnes of textiles into local landfills daily. Redress (2026) puts that in perspective: it's roughly the weight of 17,000 suitcases of clothing, every single day.

Globally, the fashion industry generates over 120 million tonnes of textile waste a year and accounts for 2–8% of global greenhouse-gas emissions (United Nations Environment Programme, 2023; Redress, 2026).

What we're
doing about it

Three connected activities, in this order.

  1. 1

    A collection booth

    Students and staff donate unwanted garments on campus — or submit them online first so we can review before drop-off.

  2. 2

    Upcycling workshops

    An introduction to fast fashion, sustainability and reuse. Participants personalise one of their own items with simple embroidery or patches, then keep it or donate it for redistribution.

  3. 3

    A free campus rack

    Collected garments are offered to students and staff free of charge, with optional contributions. Anything left is passed to a partner NGO for the people and groups it supports.

The goals
we work toward

ThreadForward is mapped to four of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals.

4Quality
education

Quality Education

Our workshops teach participants about fast fashion and sustainability alongside a practical upcycling activity — learning that reaches beyond the academic curriculum.

10Reduced
inequalities

Reduced Inequalities

Offering usable clothing free of charge, and passing remaining items to a partner NGO, makes good clothing more accessible to people who need it.

12Responsible consumption & production

Responsible Consumption & Production

We encourage reuse, repair and longer use of clothing, and help participants reflect on their own consumption habits.

13Climate
action

Climate Action

Promoting textile reuse, waste diversion and upcycling tackles fashion's carbon footprint. Extending a garment's life avoids the emissions and resources needed to make a new one, building sustainable habits across the university community.

How we
count impact

We only count a piece once it has been collected by its next owner — not when it's donated. Weight and water figures use a fixed estimate per category (for example, roughly 0.8 kg and 7,500 L for a pair of jeans) compared with making the same garment new.

These are estimates, and we label them that way everywhere they appear. As the project grows we'll weigh donations at the booth and publish our method.
